    Electric vehicle battery hijack simulation

    01 / Normal drive

    A normal drive.

    The pack holds its thermal margin. The one-RC model predicts cell temperature from current and ambient conditions, and the filter agrees with the sensors.

    Simulated telemetry. Speeds and temperatures are generated, not measured.

    Scroll to begin

    02 / Intrusion

    A forged command reaches the bus.

    The attacker spoofs the battery telemetry channel. The car accelerates under commands the driver never asked for, and reported temperature begins to drift away from the physical model.

    Attack model: thermal sensor spoofing plus a manipulated current request.

    03 / Vehicle response

    The cabin turns red.

    Internal alarms fire once the filter raises suspicion. The vehicle derates traction and isolates the suspect telemetry channel.

    Red alarms follow the EKF verdict, not the spoofed reading. Warning is simulated at 50 °C and hazard at 55 °C.

    04 / EKF

    The filter sees the lie.

    The extended Kalman filter compares reported temperature with its physical prediction. Innovation persists until the classifier moves from safe to suspect to confirmed malware.

    Classification uses a rolling window of innovations, not a single spike.

    05 / Digital twin

    A twin takes the wheel.

    A one-RC digital twin is initialised from validated parameters. It can answer the attacker without exposing the real pack.

    Twin state: state of charge, polarization voltage, and cell temperature.

    06 / Deception

    Poisoned answers.

    The twin returns fabricated temperature and state of charge. The attacker believes the pack is healthy. Then the channel goes dark.

    A bounded set of fabricated packets is sent, then the twin stops responding.

    07 / Rest mode

    The vehicle comes to rest.

    Traction stops, contactors open, and the simulation settles. The real pack was never exposed to the forged channel.

    End of the simulated scenario.

    End of run

    What the simulation showed.

    A forged thermal channel moved a reported temperature away from the physics of the pack. A model-based filter caught the disagreement, a digital twin answered the attacker with fabricated data, and the vehicle stopped.

    Detection

    The EKF tracks state of charge, polarization voltage, and temperature. The classifier needs sustained strong residuals against the independent one-RC prediction before it confirms an attack.

    Response

    Confirmation seeds a one-RC twin from the filter estimate. The twin serves 12 fabricated packets, then goes silent. Rest mode follows at 11:00 simulated.

    Limits

    This is a teaching model, not a vehicle interface. Parameters mirror configs/default.yaml. No hardware is connected and no real telemetry is read.
